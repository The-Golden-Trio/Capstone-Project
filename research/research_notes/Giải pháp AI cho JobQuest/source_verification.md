### Inferences
- The JudgeBench claim in the design argument should cite the sentence about the solver/judge ablation and say "highly correlated" as the authors' description; it is a comparison over four models, not a statistical correlation.
- Preference Leakage supports "avoid judging with a model related to the one that produced the training or generation data"; it does not measure a judge scoring its own raw output. For the project, the closest reading is the "same family" rows: same series 8.9%, different series 2.8%.
- FaithBench was built from samples where detectors disagree, so its near-chance accuracies describe hard cases only; MiniCheck's 74.7% on LLM-AggreFact (already [A+]) and its 50.50–55.03% here are not in conflict.
- Jung et al. gives the cleanest support for "model size matters": the same rubric and data, small model ICC < 0.20 with humans, large model 0.41–0.57.

### Gaps
- FaithBench's publication venue was not checked beyond the arXiv listing.
- The PMLR page range for Goel et al. comes from a search summary; the volume number (267) was read on the page.
