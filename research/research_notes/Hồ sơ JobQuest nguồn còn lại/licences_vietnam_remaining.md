## 1. O\*NET: which licence covers the Mini-IP, translations, the database files, and use outside the US

### Takeaway
The Mini-IP is covered by the O\*NET Career Exploration Tools licence page, which offers two licences: CC BY-ND 4.0 for verbatim copies, and the O\*NET Tools Developer License for anything modified, which the page itself summarises as "validate all products incorporating content from the Tools". A translation is a modification, so the proposal's conclusion (write the cards from scratch) holds; the only correction is that the tools are not CC BY 4.0. The database files used for matching are plain CC BY 4.0 with a fixed credit line. No official statement on translation into other languages or on use outside the US was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Two licences, chosen by intended use; the earlier note's "CC BY 4.0 plus a developer licence" is wrong for the tools.** The licence page for the tools reads: "The O\*NET Career Exploration Tools (including the O\*NET Interest Profiler) are available under two licenses, with different rights and limitations. You may redistribute the tools under whichever license best fits your intended use. If you want to copy or publish the Tools verbatim, use the CC BY-ND 4.0 license. If you want to modify, edit, or extend the Tools, use the O\*NET Tools Developer License." **[read primary text, raw text]** — [O\*NET® Career Exploration Tools Content License](https://www.onetcenter.org/license_tools.html)
- **Option 1 (verbatim copy) is No-Derivatives.** "The contents of the O\*NET Career Exploration Tools are licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution-NoDerivatives 4.0 International License. You are free to copy and redistribute these tools, as long as you: credit the O\*NET Career Exploration Tools and the U.S. Department of Labor, Employment and Training Administration as the original source, link to the license, and do not redistribute any modified or extended versions of the tools." Credit line: "This page includes information from the O\*NET Career Exploration Tools by the U.S. Department of Labor, Employment and Training Administration (USDOL/ETA). Used under the CC BY-ND 4.0 license. O\*NET® is a trademark of USDOL/ETA." **[read primary text, raw text]** — [same page](https://www.onetcenter.org/license_tools.html)
- **Option 2 (modify or extend) carries a validation condition, stated on the summary page too.** "Developers and researchers are free to modify, edit, extend, or create new tools based upon the O\*NET Career Exploration Tools, as long as they: credit the O\*NET Career Exploration Tools and the U.S. Department of Labor, Employment and Training Administration as the original source, link to this license page, and **validate all products incorporating content from the Tools**." Credit line: "This page includes information from the O\*NET Career Exploration Tools by the U.S. Department of Labor, Employment and Training Administration (USDOL/ETA). Used under the O\*NET Tools Developer License. O\*NET® is a trademark of USDOL/ETA. [Your name or company] has modified all or some of this information. USDOL/ETA has not approved, endorsed, or tested these modifications." **[read primary text, raw text]** — [same page](https://www.onetcenter.org/license_tools.html)
- **Is the Mini-IP covered? Yes, through the Interest Profiler page.** "License Exceptions. The licenses on this page apply only to the online and downloadable tools described on the following pages: Interest Profiler; Work Importance Locator (retired, materials archived); Ability Profiler (retired, materials archived); Interest Profiler Long Form (retired, no longer distributed); Computerized Interest Profiler (retired, no longer distributed); Work Importance Profiler (retired, no longer distributed)." **[read primary text, raw text]** — [same page](https://www.onetcenter.org/license_tools.html). The Interest Profiler page it points to describes both the Mini-IP (30 questions) and the Short Form (60 questions), with the row "Developer licensing and documentation: Yes" for both, and repeats: "The O\*NET Interest Profiler may be redistributed or used to develop other assessments under a choice of licenses. See the Career Exploration Tools License page for more information." **[read primary text, raw text for the licence sentences; table via fetch tool]** — [Interest Profiler (IP) at O\*NET Resource Center](https://www.onetcenter.org/IP.html)
- **Trap on the Interest Profiler page.** Its footer says "Interest Profiler (IP) by U.S. Department of Labor, Employment and Training Administration is licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International License." That line is the licence of the web page's own text; the tool content is governed by the two-licence page above. This is the probable origin of the earlier note's "CC BY 4.0". **[read primary text, raw text]** — [same page](https://www.onetcenter.org/IP.html)
- **Translation.** The licence page for the tools has no sentence about translation. The Developer License's definition of Adapted Material names it: material "in which the Licensed Material is translated, altered, arranged, transformed, or otherwise modified". Under CC BY-ND a translation is an adaptation and may not be shared. So a Vietnamese Mini-IP can only be distributed under the Developer License, with its validation and notice conditions. **[official page via fetch tool for the Developer License; the 3(b) wording was read in full text in the earlier round, see `source_verification.md` item 24]** — [O\*NET Tools Developer License](https://www.onetcenter.org/license_toolsdev.html)
- **Territory.** The Developer License grants "a worldwide, royalty-free, non-sublicensable, non-exclusive, irrevocable license". Neither licence page has any sentence restricting or addressing use outside the United States. **[official page via fetch tool]** — [O\*NET Tools Developer License](https://www.onetcenter.org/license_toolsdev.html)
- **Official languages.** The Interest Profiler page lists English and Spanish only (Mini-IP: widget, free web version and web-service API in both; Short Form: API in both). The "Developing a Spanish Language Version" section only points to Mi Próximo Paso and the web services; it does not describe a translation or validation procedure. **[official page via fetch tool]** — [Interest Profiler page](https://www.onetcenter.org/IP.html)
- **Database files (occupation interest scores, work styles): plain CC BY 4.0.** Page title "O\*NET® 31.0 Database Content License"; licence "Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International License". Required credit, unmodified: "This page includes information from the O\*NET 31.0 Database by the U.S. Department of Labor, Employment and Training Administration (USDOL/ETA). Used under the CC BY 4.0 license. O\*NET® is a trademark of USDOL/ETA." If modified, add: "[Your name or company] has modified all or some of this information. USDOL/ETA has not approved, endorsed, or tested these modifications." Trademark rule: "The trademark symbol must be properly displayed when referring to O\*NET." No sentence about translation or about use outside the US. **[official page via fetch tool; re-open to copy the credit block exactly]** — [O\*NET 31.0 Database Content License](https://www.onetcenter.org/license_db.html)

### Inferences
- The proposal's licence reasoning stands and is now on firmer ground: the *summary* page itself says "validate all products incorporating content from the Tools", so the validation duty does not depend on a fine reading of section 3(b)(2).
- One correction to carry into the proposal and the thesis: the tools are **CC BY-ND 4.0 or the O\*NET Tools Developer License**, not CC BY 4.0. CC BY 4.0 is the licence of the **database** (and of the web page text).
- The matching step (22 roles × six interest scores, work styles) uses database files, so it needs only the CC BY 4.0 credit line plus the "has modified" sentence, because the team re-centres and aggregates the scores. No validation condition attaches to the database.
- Self-written cards that do not translate or paraphrase Mini-IP items are outside both tool licences. Whether "written from the RIASEC definitions" could still be argued to be "based upon" the tool is a legal question; the safe practice is to write from Holland's construct definitions and not to have the Mini-IP item list open while writing.

### Gaps
- No official O\*NET statement about translations into languages other than Spanish, or about non-US deployments, was found on the pages opened. Absence on four pages, not proof that none exists; the team can ask onet@onetcenter.org.
- The size or design of a "Validation Study" is not specified beyond the reference to the *Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing* and 41 C.F.R. Part 60-3 (earlier round).

## 2. Personality and adaptability instruments: IPIP, BFI-2, HEXACO, TIPI, CAAS

### Takeaway
Two of the five were read on the authors' own pages. HEXACO is free only for non-profit academic research and may not be put in front of the general public online, which rules it out for a public web game; TIPI may be used by anyone for any purpose but has no Vietnamese version listed. IPIP and BFI-2 terms could again not be opened at source and rest on search-engine excerpts of the official pages; CAAS terms were not found at all.

### Cited Findings
- **HEXACO: free for non-profit academic research only; no public-facing online collection.** "You can download any of these forms free of charge, but only for the purpose of non-profit academic research." "Please contact the authors if you would like to use the inventory for non-academic purposes." On online use: "we do not allow researchers to collect data through an online survey site or an app accessible by the general public." Such pages "must either be password-protected or not searchable through search engines." A Vietnamese version is listed (as an Excel file only). Versions of 60, 100 and 200 items; the page states no minimum age for the HEXACO-PI-R and mentions a separate inventory for ages 10–14. **[official page via fetch tool]** — ["The HEXACO Personality Inventory - Revised"](https://hexaco.org/hexaco-inventory)
- **TIPI: unrestricted.** "WANT TO USE THE TIPI? GO AHEAD." "ANYONE CAN USE IT FOR ANY PURPOSE." "NO NEED TO ASK ME FOR PERMISSION." The page lists 26 translations; Vietnamese is not among them. On the translations: "their inclusion does not imply that I endorse them." The page also asks users to read a note on alpha reliability and factor structure before using the instrument. **[official page via fetch tool]** — ["Ten Item Personality Measure (TIPI) | Gosling"](https://gosling.psy.utexas.edu/scales-weve-developed/ten-item-personality-measure-tipi/)
- **IPIP: public domain, per a search-engine excerpt of the site's own pages; the site itself could not be opened.** The excerpt attributes to the home page: "The items and scales are in the public domain, which means that one can copy, edit, translate, or use them for any purpose without asking permission and without paying a fee." It attributes to the "Obtaining Permission" page that permission is already granted for any purpose, commercial or non-commercial, and that it is not necessary to contact the site author or the IPIP consultant (John A. Johnson). The same excerpt reports that the home page notes Lewis Goldberg's death on 29 March 2026. **[secondary source: search excerpt of the official pages; `ipip.ori.org` failed with a DNS error from the fetch tool and from `curl`, and the Wayback Machine returned HTTP 429]** — [IPIP home](https://ipip.ori.org/), ["Obtaining Permission"](https://ipip.ori.org/newPermission.htm)
- **BFI-2: copyrighted items, permission granted for personal and research use, per a search excerpt.** The excerpt of the Colby Personality Lab page says the items are copyright 2015 by Oliver P. John and Christopher J. Soto and that permission is granted for personal and research use. The word "non-commercial" was not confirmed. The same search surfaced a paper reporting 39 language versions of the BFI-2-XS produced for PIAAC ("Going Global: 39 Language Versions of the BFI-2-XS", *Measurement Instruments for the Social Sciences*); whether Vietnamese is among them was not checked. **[secondary source: search excerpt; the page returned HTTP 403 to the fetch tool and an empty body to `curl`]** — ["The Big Five Inventory–2 (BFI-2)"](https://www.colby.edu/academics/departments-and-programs/psychology/research-opportunities/personality-lab/the-bfi-2/), [Going Global paper](https://miss.psychopen.eu/index.php/miss/article/view/14067)

### Inferences
- For a public game aimed at minors, HEXACO is out on its own terms (general-public online collection is not allowed), independent of length.
- IPIP remains the only personality item pool whose terms plausibly allow translation, editing and commercial or public use, but the team should open `ipip.ori.org/newPermission.htm` in a browser and copy the sentence before citing it; this note has it only through a search excerpt.
- "Personal and research use" for BFI-2 does not obviously cover a public product; ask the authors if it is ever wanted. The proposal does not use a personality inventory, so nothing changes now.

### Gaps
- **CAAS / CAAS-SF terms: not found.** `vocopher.com` answered HTTP 404 (the home page redirects to a missing PDF). No licence sentence for the CAAS was seen anywhere; the open-access validation articles found carry CC BY for the article text only.
- IPIP and BFI-2 wording is not verified at source (see above).

## 3. Strengths: what VIA reserves; CliftonStrengths

### Takeaway
The VIA Institute's own guidelines page lists products, apps and any assessment "abridged or otherwise" as uses that need its approval, and says its measures are not recommended for selection or placement. This confirms the proposal's wording. Gallup's own pages could not be opened; what is known about CliftonStrengths is at search-excerpt level and includes an unresolved conflict over the item count.

### Cited Findings
- **VIA: what requires contacting VIA.** The page lists, among uses needing approval: "Create any products using the VIA Classification (e.g., strengths cards, strengths smart-phone apps, etc.)."; "Create an assessment – abridged or otherwise – of the VIA character strengths and virtues."; "Present an altered form of the VIA Classification (such as additional or removed strengths)"; "Reprint the VIA Classification of character strengths and virtues for any publication."; "Use the VIA logo on a website or other materials." It states that the VIA Survey, VIA Classification, VIA Reports and VIA's work products "are copyrighted material". Citing the survey or the classification in a book or article needs no approval. Contact: info@viacharacter.org. **[official page via fetch tool]** — ["Use & Interpretation Guidelines | VIA Institute"](https://www.viacharacter.org/faq/permissions-and-guidelines-for-use-and-interpretation)
- **VIA: not for selection.** "Accordingly, the VIA measures are not recommended for use in personnel selection or placement decisions." **[official page via fetch tool]** — [same page](https://www.viacharacter.org/faq/permissions-and-guidelines-for-use-and-interpretation)
- **VIA: not addressed on the page.** Games, translations and commercial use are not mentioned by name. **[official page via fetch tool]** — [same page](https://www.viacharacter.org/faq/permissions-and-guidelines-for-use-and-interpretation)
- **CliftonStrengths: a development tool, not a selection tool (excerpt level).** A search excerpt of Gallup's technical report says the instrument "is not designed or validated for use in employee selection or mental health screening"; an excerpt of Gallup's support centre says it has not been validated for selection and does not link specific themes to success in particular positions. The technical report's copyright section is reported to say it contains proprietary, copyrighted and trademarked materials of Gallup, Inc., and that no changes may be made without Gallup's written permission. **[secondary source: search excerpts; the support article returned HTTP 403]** — [Gallup support: "Can I use the CliftonStrengths assessment as a selection tool for hiring"](https://support.gallup.com/hc/en-us/articles/40531127683091-Can-I-use-the-CliftonStrengths-assessment-as-a-selection-tool-for-hiring), [The CliftonStrengths Technical Report: Development and Validation](https://www.gallup.com/services/176321/clifton-strengthsfinder-technical-report-development-validation.aspx)
- **CliftonStrengths item count: sources conflict.** An older technical report is excerpted as describing 177 stimuli; the search summary says a newer Gallup report describes a different number of paired self-descriptors. Not resolved; do not quote a count. **[secondary source]** — [same technical-report page](https://www.gallup.com/services/176321/clifton-strengthsfinder-technical-report-development-validation.aspx)
- **StrengthsExplorer** is a separate Gallup product recommended for ages 10–14, with 10 themes and a top-three report. **[secondary source: search excerpt of Gallup support]** — [Gallup support: "What is StrengthsExplorer"](https://support.gallup.com/hc/en-us/articles/52542461518483-What-is-StrengthsExplorer)

### Inferences
- A list of the 24 strengths to choose from, a strengths card set, or a shortened strengths quiz inside JobQuest would each fall under items VIA says need approval. The proposal's choice (one open question, no list, no scoring) avoids all three.
- Nothing Gallup publishes was read directly, so the thesis should not make evidence claims about CliftonStrengths beyond "proprietary and paid; the publisher itself says it is not for selection", and should cite that at the level it was read.

### Gaps
- Gallup's licence terms, price and current item count were not read from Gallup's own pages.
- Whether VIA would license a public Vietnamese web game is not stated on the page; the page directs unclear cases to its email address.

## 4. Values measures: Schwartz PVQ-RR and TwIVI; SWVI-R; MIQ

### Takeaway
Little could be raised above the earlier level. PVQ-RR has 57 items (three per each of 19 values) according to a peer-reviewed paper that used it; the stated minimum age and the licence sentence for the items were not found because Schwartz's repository PDF refused access. SWVI-R is published by Kuder, Inc. and the MIQ by Vocational Psychology Research at the University of Minnesota; licences were not read and the item counts are unsettled.

### Cited Findings
- **PVQ-RR: 57 items.** A study using the instrument describes the female form as having "57 items; 3 per value" and says the questionnaire is available at no cost for academic research, with the current version obtained from Schwartz on request. **[secondary source: search excerpt of a journal article; the article itself was not opened]** — [PMC9131418](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9131418)
- **Licence of the repository document, not of the items.** The landing page of Schwartz's repository carries a "Creative Commons Attribution-Noncommercial-No Derivative Works 3.0 License" for the repository publication. Whether that licence is meant to govern the questionnaire items is not stated in what was read. **[secondary source this round (search excerpt); the landing page was read in the earlier round]** — ["A Repository of Schwartz Value Scales with Instructions and an Introduction"](https://scholarworks.gvsu.edu/orpc/vol2/iss2/9/)
- **SWVI-R: publisher and scales.** The Buros listing gives the authors as Donald E. Super and Donald G. Zytowski, the publisher as Kuder, Inc., and the dates as 2001–2015; it notes that the publisher has not supplied materials for review of the 2015 revision. Twelve scales: Achievement, Challenge, Co-Workers, Creativity, Income, Independence, Lifestyle, Prestige, Security, Supervision, Variety, Workplace. **[secondary source: search excerpt of the Buros listing]** — [Buros: Super's Work Values Inventory—Revised](https://marketplace.unl.edu/buros/super-s-work-values-inventory-revised.html)
- **MIQ: structure.** Measures 20 vocational needs grouped into six values; a paired form (choose the more important need in each pair) and a ranked form (rank needs within blocks), the ranked form adding a 21st need; described as suitable for a fifth-grade reading level. **[secondary source: search excerpts of the VPR page and an encyclopedia entry; `vpr.psych.umn.edu/miq` returned HTTP 403]** — [(MIQ) Minnesota Importance Questionnaire, Vocational Psychology Research](https://vpr.psych.umn.edu/miq)

### Inferences
- The proposal uses no values scale (one open question, quoted back), so none of these terms binds the current design. They matter only if a later version wants a scored values block.
- A CC BY-NC-ND licence on the repository, if it does extend to the items, would bar both a derived short form and commercial use; that is a further reason not to build on PVQ items without writing to Schwartz.

### Gaps
- **PVQ-RR stated minimum age: not found.** The repository PDF returned HTTP 403 in both rounds.
- **TwIVI / TIVI terms: not found.** The page on the Gosling lab site returned nothing usable.
- **SWVI-R item count: sources conflict** (a Sage encyclopedia excerpt says four statements per scale, which would give 48; the earlier notes carried 72). Neither was verified against Kuder's manual. Licence and price not read.
- **MIQ item count and licence: not found.** "190 pairs" is arithmetic from 20 needs (20 × 19 ÷ 2), not a sourced figure; do not cite it.

## 5. My Career Story workbook: copyright and permitted use

### Takeaway
The workbook is copyrighted by its two authors and offered as a free printable download; no reuse or translation licence was found, and the existing translations were made with the authors' permission. Using its questions verbatim or translating it needs a written request.

### Cited Findings

Hartung and says "a printable PDF version of this workbook may be downloaded at www.vocopher.com". **[secondary source: search excerpt of the workbook description hosted on the first author's site]** — [MCS Description (marksavickas.com)](https://www.marksavickas.com/files/Assessments/My%20Career%20Story%20Workbook/1.%20MCS_Description.pdf)

### Gaps

## 7. Origin of the ikigai Venn diagram

### Takeaway
Marc Winn's own account was read: in 2014 he took an existing Venn diagram about "purpose", changed one word to "ikigai", and published it. That is a primary source for his half of the story. *The Economist* could not be opened, and Andrés Zuzunaga's authorship of the earlier diagram is not confirmed from any primary source here.

### Cited Findings
- **Winn, in his own words.** "In 2014, I wrote a blog post on the subject of Ikigai." "I merged a venn diagram on 'purpose' with Dan Buettner's Ikigai concept". "I changed one word on a diagram and shared a 'new' meme with the world." He puts the effort at under an hour. **[read primary text through a fetch tool: the author's own blog post, dated 25 October 2017]** — ["Meme Seeding", Marc Winn](https://theviewinside.me/meme-seeding/)
- **Zuzunaga is not named in the post.** In a 2019 reply to a reader's comment on the same page, Winn writes that "over the years the crowd has indicated that this is indeed the original source of the venn diagram on purpose"; the fetch tool reports that other commenters dispute the attribution and that Winn says he cannot remember where he found the diagram. **[official page via fetch tool; the comment thread was summarised, not read line by line]** — [same page](https://theviewinside.me/meme-seeding/)

### Inferences
- The proposal's claim "the four-circle diagram does not come from Japan" can now cite Winn directly instead of Wikipedia: the person who attached the word says he did so in 2014, in Guernsey, to a Western "purpose" diagram.
- Attribution of the "purpose" diagram to Zuzunaga (commonly dated 2011) should be written as "widely attributed to", with a secondary citation, until someone opens Zuzunaga's own page.

### Gaps
- ***The Economist* (2022) was not opened**: the fetch tool refuses the domain. Its content is not reported here.
- Zuzunaga's own publication of the diagram and its date were not opened.

## 11. Vietnamese legal duties when a free-text box may receive a minor's disclosure of harm

## 14. Typing Vietnamese on phones

### Inferences
- Vietnamese with diacritics needs more keystrokes per word than the English text in the study above, so the English figure is at best an upper bound for a guess. Time per open answer on a phone has to come from the team's own pilot.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or industry measurement for Vietnamese was found in one English-language search; a Vietnamese-language search and the HCI venues (CHI, MobileHCI) were not searched directly.
