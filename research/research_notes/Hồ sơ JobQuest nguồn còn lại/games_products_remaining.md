### Inferences

- The Sims gives a second model for the constellation layer besides Animal Crossing: the sign is a *label derived from* what the player set (points in, sign out), is shown changing live while the player edits, and stops updating after creation. - Final Fantasy Tactics: a reviewer who has replayed the game says he never took note of the mechanic, and a 2026 news article still calls it "hidden". Two sources are thin, but both point the same way as the proposal's "avoid mechanics the player cannot see".
- Telltale is the one case where a developer (Iggo) is quoted on what aggregate choice statistics showed; the Wikipedia account adds that the studio used the tracking to rewrite later dialogue. The statistic served the designers at least as much as the players.

## 2. Developer talks, motivation models and avatar-identification papers

### Takeaway
No talk, post-mortem or long interview on quiz-based character creation or first-session length was found in this round either. Quantic Foundry's own page confirms the method (factor analysis) but gives a smaller sample than the figure quoted in round 1, and the two avatar-identification papers are now read at abstract level.

### Cited Findings
- **Quantic Foundry, own page**: "Using a factor analysis on data collected from over 140,000 gamers, we identified how these motivations clustered together. Motivations in the same column are more highly related than motivations in different columns." "Across all the geographic regions we had data for, the same high-level motivation structure emerged, revealing 3 clusters of motivations." "Fill out a 5-minute survey and get your personalized gaming motivation report." "When gamers complete the motivation profile tool, we also ask them to list their favorite games." "Quantic Foundry is a market research company focused on gamer motivation." The names and number of the motivations are in a graphic, not in the page text. — [Quantic Foundry, "Gamer Motivation Model"](https://quanticfoundry.com/gamer-motivation-model/) [read page via browser; vendor page]
- Round 1 figure for comparison: "12 distinct motives belonging to six higher-order motivation clusters", "tested on more than 500,000 gamers worldwide". — [Király et al. (2022), Journal of Behavioral Addictions 11(3)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9872527) [read page, round 1]
- **Turkay & Kinzer**, abstract as shown on the reprint page (chapter in *Gamification: Concepts, Methodologies, Tools, and Applications*; Selen Turkay, Harvard University, and Charles K. Kinzer, Columbia University): "This mixed method study aims to fill the gap in the identification literature by examining the effects of avatar-based customization on players' identification with and empathy towards their characters in a massively multiplayer online game, Lord of the Rings Online (LotRO). Participants (N = 66) played LotRO either in customization or in no-customization groups for about ten hours in four sessions over two weeks in a controlled lab setting. Data were collected through interviews, surveys and observations. Results showed both time and avatar-based customization positively impacted players' identification with their avatars. Self-Determination Theory is used to interpret results." — [IRMA International chapter page](https://www.irma-international.org/chapter/the-effects-of-avatar-based-customization-on-player-identification/126061/) [abstract / description only, read via browser]
- **Birk, Atkins, Bowey & Mandryk (2016)**, "Fostering Intrinsic Motivation through Avatar Identification in Digital Games", CHI '16, pp. 2982–2995, DOI 10.1145/2858036.2858062. Abstract as reconstructed from the OpenAlex index record (paraphrase by the fetch tool, not verbatim): 126 participants played a custom endless-runner game; similarity, embodied and wishful identification each increased autonomy, immersion, invested effort, enjoyment and positive affect; higher identification also produced more motivated behaviour, measured as time spent in an unending version of the game. — [OpenAlex record for the DOI](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1145/2858036.2858062) [abstract / description only; index record, paraphrased]
- **Silent Hill: Shattered Memories**: a search summary again surfaced an interview with Sam Barlow and Tomm Hulett (team put through personality tests; five-factor model with one factor dropped). The only copies returned are blog reposts on unrelated domains; not opened. [secondary / snippet; same item round 2 marked "do not cite"]

### Inferences
- The two Quantic Foundry sample figures are not in conflict if read as different moments: the vendor page describes the factor analysis that produced the clusters (over 140,000), Király et al. describe the total tested (over 500,000). The report should quote one figure with its source, not merge them.
- Both avatar papers are lab studies of *avatars* (a customised character; an endless-runner avatar). They support "letting the player shape the thing that represents them raises identification and motivation"; they do not test a text profile or a questionnaire, so they are background for the profile page, not evidence for Get to know me.
