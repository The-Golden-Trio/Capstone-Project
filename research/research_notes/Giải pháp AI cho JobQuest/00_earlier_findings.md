## Pass 1. Does the judge need to be stronger than the generator? (7 Oct)

Findings:

- When a judge evaluates another model's output WITHOUT a reference, it agrees with experts mainly on items it could answer correctly itself; an expert-written reference largely removes this dependence. Krumdick, Lovering, Reddy, Ebner, Tanner, "No Free Labels: Limitations of LLM-as-a-Judge Without Human Grounding", arXiv:2503.05061 (preprint). [A+] Table 4, pairwise grading, GPT-4o as judge: kappa 0.78 on questions it answered correctly and 0.30 on questions it answered incorrectly with no reference; 0.86 and 0.16 with a self-generated reference; 0.92 and 0.83 with a human reference. Qwen 2.5 7B with a human reference: 0.78. (An agent misreported this as "0.78 to 0.14"; the figures here are the corrected reading.)
- JudgeBench (Tan et al., ICLR 2025, arXiv:2410.12784; already cited in the project report as [60]) [U for numbers]: judging accuracy correlates with the model's own solving accuracy; GPT-4o 50.65, o1-preview 75.43, Claude-3.5-Sonnet 64.29 on pairwise correctness.
- Small purpose-built checkers given the source text approach the largest models: Tang, Laban, Durrett, "MiniCheck: Efficient Fact-Checking of LLMs on Grounding Documents", EMNLP 2024, arXiv:2404.10774. [A+] Table 2, LLM-AggreFact balanced accuracy: MiniCheck-FT5 74.7, GPT-4 75.3, Claude-3 Opus 74.1, AlignScore 70.4. Table 3: $0.24 vs $107 on the test set. MiniCheck-FT5 has 770M parameters.
- No checker is reliable on subtle inconsistencies: Bao et al., "FaithBench", arXiv:2410.13210 (preprint) [A abstract; U numbers]: best detectors 54–58% balanced accuracy on hard summaries.
- Judges favour their own and related models' outputs: Panickssery, Bowman, Feng, "LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations", arXiv:2404.13076 (agent says NeurIPS 2024; venue [U]) [A]; Li et al., "Preference Leakage: A Contamination Problem in LLM-as-a-judge", ICLR 2026, arXiv:2502.01534 [A; table numbers U: same model 23.6%, same family same series 8.9%, same family different series 2.8%]; Goel et al., "Great Models Think Alike and this Undermines AI Oversight", arXiv:2502.04313 (agent says ICML 2025; venue [U]) [A]: judge scores favour models whose mistakes resemble the judge's, and mistakes become more similar as capability rises.
- Panels: Verga et al., "Replacing judges with juries", arXiv:2404.18796 (preprint; cited in the report as [74]). [A+] The panel is Command R (35B), Claude Haiku and GPT-3.5; settings are binary QA correctness and Chatbot Arena pairwise scoring; Cohen's kappa 0.763 / 0.906 / 0.867 for the panel vs 0.627 / 0.841 / 0.830 for GPT-4 (Table 1); Pearson 0.917 vs 0.817 (Table 2); seven to eight times cheaper.
- Counter-evidence on panels: Kohli, "Nine Judges, Two Effective Votes: Correlated Errors Undermine LLM Evaluation Panels", arXiv:2605.29800 (preprint, 2026) [A]: 9 judges from 7 families on NLI give about 2 independent votes; the best single judge matches or beats the panel. Mukherjee, Hamna, Bali, Sitaram, "The Geometry of LLM-as-Judge: Why Inter-LLM Consensus Is Not Human Alignment", EMNLP 2026, arXiv:2606.03043 [A]: 42 judges, 8 Indic languages; judges agree with each other as much as humans do but reach 58–66% of human agreement; ensembles converge on the judges' shared axis.
- Reasoning models are not a safe upgrade for ordinal scoring: Yoshida, "Are the Reasoning Models Good at Automated Essay Scoring?", Findings of EMNLP 2025, pp. 8388–8394, doi:10.18653/v1/2025.findings-emnlp.445 [A]: o3-mini and o4-mini had significantly lower validity than GPT-4o mini on TOEFL11; ICC about 0.7 vs 0.95; few-shot helped more than chain of thought.
- Model size matters for alignment with humans: Jung, Lu, Benker, Darici, "How Model Size, Temperature, and Prompt Style Affect LLM-Human Assessment Score Alignment", NCME AIME 2025, arXiv:2509.19329 [A; ICC numbers U]. Mansour et al., "Can Large Language Models Automatically Score Proficiency of Written Essays?", LREC-COLING 2024, arXiv:2403.06149 [A; QWK numbers U].
- Fine-tuned open judge: Kim et al., "Prometheus: Inducing Fine-grained Evaluation Capability in Language Models", ICLR 2024, arXiv:2310.08491 [A]: on par with GPT-4 when given a reference answer and score rubric.
- Cascade: Jung, Brahman, Choi, "Trust or Escalate: LLM Judges with Provable Guarantees for Human Agreement", arXiv:2407.18370 (agent says ICLR 2025; venue [U]) [A]: cheap judges first, escalate to a stronger one when confidence is low; pairwise setting.
- Already in the project report: Zheng et al. 2023 [57]; Liu et al. G-Eval 2023 [58]; "Reliability without validity", arXiv:2606.19544 [59]; RewardBench [64]; Sai et al. 2021 perturbation checklists [73]; Li et al. essay-scoring synthesis arXiv:2512.14561 [101]; Henkel et al. 2024 [102]; Yancey et al. 2023 [103]; Stahl et al. 2024 [104]; Hackl et al. 2023 [105]; Williamson, Xi and Breyer 2012 [71].

Not answered by the literature: no study of LLM rubric scoring of Vietnamese answers or of occupational tasks; small vs large judge on a four-level ordinal scale; whether a panel median beats its best member on ordinal scoring.

Conclusion adopted: the judge's strength is not measured against the generator. For scoring human answers (AI-5) what matters is agreement with experts under an explicit rubric with scored examples; panel composition is settled by measurement. For checking generated text against a seed (offline, in AI-3) the checker need not be stronger because the seed is the reference, but it should come from a different family.

## Pass 2. Software frameworks for an LLM judge (7 Oct)

About 20 candidates were examined from official docs, repositories and papers.

- No library provides the project's judge design out of the box (three providers, one structured call per judge, ordinal multi-competency rubric, median, flag on spread). None computes quadratic weighted kappa or Krippendorff's alpha; scikit-learn (`cohen_kappa_score(weights="quadratic")`) and the `krippendorff` package are needed regardless.
- Most tools have no peer-reviewed paper; the citable science is in the methods (G-Eval, panels, Prometheus, LLM-Rubric), not the tooling.
- Recommended for the offline harness: Inspect AI (UK AI Security Institute with Meridian Labs, MIT licence, version 0.3.277 dated 6 Oct 2026 in the changelog). [A, via official source and docs] `model_graded_qa(model=[...])` builds a `multi_scorer` with default reducer "majority"; the built-in grader extracts a single grade by regex, so a multi-competency rubric needs a custom scorer; `Score` carries value, explanation, metadata; dict-valued scores are named by key; `Epochs` supports reducers. [U] median reduction across graders; structured output (`--response-schema`) said to be limited to OpenAI, Google and Mistral.
- Recommended at run time: no judge framework; a thin TypeScript module on the Vercel AI SDK (`generateText` with `Output.object({schema})` and zod; `result.usage` for tokens) [A]. Runner-up openevals-js (LangChain).
- Rejected: OpenAI Evals (OpenAI's cookbook says the product is winding down [U]); HELM and lm-evaluation-harness (model benchmarking, not rubric judging); Prometheus-eval and Verdict (no releases since 2024–2025 [U]); DeepEval and Ragas (1–5 scale normalised to 0–1, one judge per metric [U]); promptfoo (0–1 scores [U]); Vertex AI, Azure and Bedrock evaluation services (paid, cloud-locked [U]).
- Evidence that Inspect is used in research [U, search result only]: "Developing and Maintaining an Open-Source Repository of AI Evaluations: Challenges and Insights", arXiv:2507.06893.
- LLM-Rubric: Hashemi, Eisner, Rosset, Van Durme, Kedzie, "LLM-Rubric: A Multidimensional, Calibrated Approach to Automated Evaluation of Natural Language Texts", ACL 2024, pp. 13806–13834, arXiv:2501.00274 [A]: a small network calibrates LLM rubric answers to individual human judges; RMS error below 0.5 on a 1–4 scale, about twice as accurate as the uncalibrated baseline.

## Pass 3. AI-4, seed-constrained run-time narration and follow-ups (8 Oct)

Recommended design points and their support:

1. The engine owns the plot; the model fills the gaps. Riedl and Bulitko, "Interactive Narrative: An Intelligent Systems Approach", AI Magazine 34(1), 2013, doi:10.1609/aimag.v34i1.2449 [A]: experience managers, plot graphs with precedence constraints, intervention vs accommodation when the player deviates. LLM systems keeping author structure outside free generation: Wu et al., "From Role-Play to Drama-Interaction", arXiv:2405.14231 (preprint) [U]; Sun et al., "Drama Llama", arXiv:2501.09099 (preprint) [U]: LLM-checked natural-language triggers were inconsistent; Wang et al., "Dramamancer", arXiv:2601.18785 (workshop abstract) [U]. No LLM system was found that reports a quantitative adherence rate to authored structure.
2. Handle off-script input by deflecting in character. Mehta, Dow, Mateas, MacIntyre, "Evaluating a Conversation-Centered Interactive Drama", AAMAS 2007 [U]: Façade, 12 players; "not understood" outcomes broke the experience less than misunderstood input; echoing a shallow understanding hurt.
3. Put the whole seed in the prompt; no RAG or fine-tuning. Li, Li, Zhang, Mei, Bendersky, "Retrieval Augmented Generation or Long-Context LLMs? A Comprehensive Study and Hybrid Approach", EMNLP 2024 industry track, arXiv:2407.16833 [A]: long context consistently outperforms RAG on average when resourced sufficiently; RAG is much cheaper. Ovadia et al., arXiv:2312.05934 [U]: RAG beat fine-tuning for injecting facts; fine-tuning inconsistent. Liu et al., "Lost in the Middle", TACL 2024, doi:10.1162/tacl_a_00638 [U]. Applying these to a 1–2k-token closed seed is an inference.
4. Structured output sparingly, free-text field first. Tam et al., "Let Me Speak Freely? A Study on the Impact of Format Restrictions on Performance of Large Language Models", arXiv:2408.02442 (agent says EMNLP 2024 industry; venue [U]) [A]: format restrictions significantly reduce reasoning performance; stricter constraints, greater degradation. Geng et al., EMNLP 2023, doi:10.18653/v1/2023.emnlp-main.674 [U]: grammar-constrained decoding guarantees validity but needs logits.
5. Run-time rule checks from the seed. Thomson and Reiter, "A Gold Standard Methodology for Evaluating Accuracy in Data-To-Text Systems", INLG 2020, arXiv:2011.03992 [A; counts U: number errors 184 of 418, name errors 105 of 418]. Nan et al., EACL 2021, doi:10.18653/v1/2021.eacl-main.235 [U]: entity "precision-source". Dodge et al., EMNLP 2021 [U]: generic blocklists remove non-offensive text.
6. Follow-ups: one goal per question, counter enforced by the engine. Xiao, Zhou, Liao, Mark, Chi, Chen, Yang, "Tell Me About Yourself: Using an AI-Powered Chatbot to Conduct Conversational Surveys with Open-ended Questions", ACM TOCHI 2020, doi:10.1145/3381804 [A]: about 600 participants, over 5,200 responses; the chatbot with follow-ups elicited more informative, relevant, specific and clear answers than a static survey. Ge et al., arXiv:2205.10977 (preprint) [U]: automatic metrics for follow-up quality correlate weakly with experts (0.16–0.37). Maurya et al., arXiv:2412.09416 (preprint) [U]: an LLM judge correlated mostly negatively with humans on "revealing the answer".
7. Adversarial input. Toyer et al., Tensor Trust, ICLR 2024, arXiv:2311.01011 [U]: 126k prompt-injection attacks; many models vulnerable. Wallace et al., arXiv:2404.13208 (preprint) [U]. Li et al., arXiv:2402.10962 [U]: persona drift within 8 rounds; repeating the system prompt helps. Tang et al., RoleBreak, arXiv:2409.16727 (preprint) [U]. Louie et al., Roleplay-doh, EMNLP 2024, arXiv:2407.00870 [U]: 20% (55/276) of base responses violated expert principles.

Waiting time: Nielsen's 0.1 s / 1 s / 10 s limits (practitioner article; primary Miller 1968 not opened) [U]; Galletta et al., JAIS 5(1), 2004, doi:10.17705/1jais.00044 [U]: intentions level off at 4 s or more, attitudes at 8 s or more; Tan, Messerschmidt, Yin, Nov, "The Impact of Response Latency and Task Type on Human-LLM Interaction and Perception", ACM CHI 2026, arXiv:2604.06183 [A]: time-to-first-token of 2, 9, 20 s; user behaviour robust to latency; 2-second responses rated less thoughtful and useful than 9- or 20-second ones; knowledge tasks, not games.

Risks that remain with rule-only checks: semantic invention built from correct tokens passes lexical checks (Thomson and Reiter shared task, arXiv:2108.05644 [U]: no automatic system detected context errors); rubric leakage through paraphrase (Zhao et al., arXiv:2604.18660, preprint [U]); authority violations and persona drift are semantic; no cited check was evaluated on Vietnamese; no published head-to-head of rule-only vs model-based checks was found.

Agent's inference, not sourced: keep the rubric and scoring keys out of AI-4's prompt; re-inject the character card every turn.

## Pass 4. AI-3, AI-assisted expert testing and triage (8 Oct)

1. Test playthroughs: LLM-simulated players with answers generated at assigned quality levels, plus a small number of real human playthroughs. Lu and Wang, "Generative Students: Using LLM-Simulated Student Profiles to Support Question Item Evaluation", L@S 2024, arXiv:2405.11591 [A]: 45 GPT-4 profiles, 20 multiple-choice items; answers correlated strongly with real students and identified the same hard questions. Limits: Balog and Bakken, "'Act Like a 5th Grader' is Not Enough: Bounding Knowledge in LLM-Based User Simulators", Findings of EMNLP 2026, arXiv:2608.30033 [A]: persona prompting gives near-perfect, uniform accuracy unlike 2,359 real students. Also [U]: Acquaye et al., arXiv:2601.09953 (simulated classrooms correlate r=0.75–0.82 with real item difficulty; weaker models predicted better); Srivatsa et al., arXiv:2507.08232; Fukuhara and Domingue 2026 (AIME-Con); Benedetto et al., Findings of EMNLP 2024, doi:10.18653/v1/2024.findings-emnlp.663; Xiao and Yang, arXiv:2410.02829 (LLM agents track relative difficulty of text games); Wang, Morgenstern, Dickerson, arXiv:2402.01908 (LLMs misportray and flatten groups). Use simulation to find relative problems, not to estimate absolute score distributions.
2. Which cases to flag: use a union of signals. Evidence is conflicting. Ferrer, Turgut, Chen, Sonkar, "When Can We Trust LLM Graders? Calibrating Confidence for Automated Assessment", arXiv:2603.29559 (preprint) [A; numbers U]: self-reported confidence best calibrated, self-consistency worse at about five times the cost, larger models better calibrated; AUC only about 0.57–0.64. Yao, Yang, Kuang 2026, AIME-Con pp. 280–287 [U]: on a 6-level rubric neither verbalised confidence nor 5-run SD flagged human–LLM mismatches. Xiong et al., ICLR 2024, arXiv:2306.13063 [U]: verbalised confidence overconfident. Wagner et al., arXiv:2410.11594 (preprint) [U]. Cong et al., arXiv:2605.00200 (AIED 2026) [U]. Funayama et al., AIED 2022, arXiv:2206.08288 [U]. Korthals et al. 2026, MAKE 8(3):74, doi:10.3390/make8030074 [U, abstract only]: flagging low-certainty answers cuts manual grading time by 40–90%. Choose thresholds from risk–coverage curves on the project's own labelled set: Geifman and El-Yaniv 2017, arXiv:1705.08500 [U]; Angelopoulos et al., Conformal Risk Control, arXiv:2208.02814 [U]; Sheng et al., EMNLP 2025, doi:10.18653/v1/2025.emnlp-main.569 [U].
3. What the expert sees: a ranked queue with the answer, the three judge scores and rationales, the flag reason and the nearest scored examples; the expert can edit a rubric anchor mid-review. Shankar, Zamfirescu-Pereira, Hartmann, Parameswaran, Arawjo, "Who Validates the Validators? Aligning LLM-Assisted Evaluation of LLM Outputs with Human Preferences", arXiv:2404.12272 (agent says UIST 2024, doi:10.1145/3654777.3676450; venue [U]) [A]: "criteria drift", users refine criteria as they grade. Kim et al., EvalLM, CHI 2024, doi:10.1145/3613904.3642216 [U].
4. Auditing unflagged cases: a random sample reviewed blind. Rule of three: 0 errors in n cases gives a 95% upper bound of about 3/n (Hanley and Lippman-Hand 1983, restated in Newman 1995, JAMA 274(13):1013) [U]. Tighter estimates: prediction-powered inference, Angelopoulos et al., arXiv:2301.09633 [U].
5. Feeding rulings back: Schleifer, Ariely, Beigman Klebanov, Salman, Alexandron, "Quality-Conditioned Agreement in Automated Short Answer Scoring: Mid-Range Degradation and the Impact of Task-Specific Adaptation", BEA 2026, arXiv:2605.07647 [A]: all AI models score fully correct and fully incorrect answers well but are much worse on partially correct ones, while human–human agreement stays constant; worst for few-shot LLMs with few examples; [U] 6–10 balanced shots perform similarly, 2 shots degrade mid-range scoring. LLM-Rubric (see Pass 2): calibration plateaus with more judgements [U].
6. Risks: confident and consistent errors are invisible to confidence signals; mid-range answers are worst; simulated runs are not exchangeable with real users; no source was found on automation bias among reviewers who see a pre-triaged queue.

## Pass 5. AI-1 expert elicitation and AI-2 seed structuring (8 Oct)

AI-1:

1. Fixed, human-authored topic guide with LLM probing inside each topic. Chopra and Haaland, "Conducting Qualitative Interviews with AI", CESifo Working Paper 10666 (working paper) [A]: AI-led interviews collect qualitative data at scale with high quality; adaptive follow-ups surface themes otherwise missed. [U] Table 1: of over 8,000 hand-coded question–answer pairs, 95–99.8% open-ended, 95.7–99.6% neutral, 0.1–0.4% hallucinated questions; 766 interviews; the interviewee is shown a summary and asked to confirm it.
2. Probing is the weak point. Wuttke, Aßenmacher, Klamm, Lang, Würschinger, Kreuter, "AI Conversational Interviewing: Transforming Surveys with LLMs as Adaptive Interviewers", LaTeCH-CLfL 2025, arXiv:2410.01824 [A]: comparable data quality to human interviewers in a small controlled study with students. [U]: 5 AI vs 6 human interviews; the AI committed 88% of follow-up violations (under-probing); prompt sensitivity.
3. Anchor on a specific incident: Flanagan 1954 (in the report as [26]); critical decision method, Klein, Calderwood, MacGregor 1989, IEEE Trans. SMC 19(3):462–472, doi:10.1109/21.31053 [U, metadata only]; applied cognitive task analysis, Militello and Hutton 1998, Ergonomics 41(11):1618–1641, doi:10.1080/001401398186108 [U, abstract only].
4. Ask for concrete tasks and behaviours: Morgeson et al. 2004, Journal of Applied Psychology 89(4):674–686 [U]: incumbents inflate ability statements more than task statements.
5. Coverage tracking, summarise-and-confirm, verbatim provenance. Korn, Gorsch, Vogelsang, "LLMREI: Automating Requirements Elicitation Interviews with LLMs", arXiv:2507.02564 (preprint) [A]: 33 simulated interviews; about as many errors as human interviewers; extracts a large share of requirements. [U]: at most 60.9% fully elicited, 73.7% including partial; it invented a project price. Birt et al. 2016, Qualitative Health Research 26(13):1802–1811, doi:10.1177/1049732316654870 [U]: member checking.
6. Several experts: Delphi, Hasson, Keeney, McKenna 2000, Journal of Advanced Nursing 32(4):1008–1015, doi:10.1046/j.1365-2648.2000.01567.x [U]. Tessler et al. 2024, Science 386:eadq2852, doi:10.1126/science.adq2852 [U]: AI-mediated group statements preferred over human mediators'. LLM summarisers smooth disagreement: Aghaebe et al., arXiv:2601.04889 (preprint) [U]; Wang et al., Findings of EMNLP 2025, doi:10.18653/v1/2025.findings-emnlp.876 [U].

AI-2:

1. ECD-style schema: Mislevy and Haertel 2006, Educational Measurement: Issues and Practice 25(4):6–20 [U]: PADI design templates with task-model variables, evidence rules, shared data structures and authoring wizards.
2. Generate from verbatim incident text: Liu et al., AHFE, doi:10.54941/ahfe1007533 [U]: SJT items from critical-incident transcripts with RAG and expert review; no numbers.
3. Reason in free text, then convert to the schema: Tam et al. (see Pass 3).
4. Validate deterministically and repair: Rogosch and Schrader 2026, Applied Sciences 16(6):2932, doi:10.3390/app16062932 [U, page returned 403]: 68–86% of 240 seeds compiled, were playable and faithful; repair iterations central.
5. Experts own every key and rubric anchor. Wu, Saraf, Lee, Latif, Liu, Zhai, "Unveiling Scoring Processes: Dissecting the Differences between LLMs and Human Graders in Automatic Scoring", arXiv:2407.18328 (accepted, Technology, Knowledge and Learning) [A]: LLM-generated rubrics differ from human ones and LLMs rely on shortcuts; giving them analytic rubrics that reflect human grading logic narrows the gap. Klang et al. 2023, BMC Medical Education 23:772, doi:10.1186/s12909-023-04752-w [U]: about 15% of 210 GPT-4 multiple-choice questions needed revision, 0.5% false. Also [U]: Law et al. 2025, BMC Medical Education 25:208; Siro et al., arXiv:2602.08672 (EACL 2026 Findings); Yost et al., arXiv:2510.22170 (preprint); Mittelstädt et al. 2024, Scientific Reports 14:27449; Li et al., arXiv:2412.12144.
6. Homogenisation and social desirability [U]: Salecha et al., PNAS Nexus 2024, arXiv:2405.06058; Anderson et al., C&C 2024, arXiv:2402.01536.

Gaps: no study of an LLM conducting critical-incident or job-analysis interviews with domain experts; nothing for IT occupations, Vietnamese, or time-limited volunteer experts; no measured expert revision effort for LLM-drafted seeds with ordinal anchors.

## Precedent: another capstone under the same advisor

"aBridgeAI: An AI-Powered Career Readiness Learning Management System", HCMUT capstone report, September 2026 (advisor Assoc. Prof. Võ Thị Ngọc Châu; students Lê Thanh Duy, Võ Hoàng Ngân, Nguyễn Hoàng Nam). The coordinator read about 30 of its 493 pages.

- Section 7.4 selects the quiz-generation model by measurement: three candidate models, 2 lessons × 12 questions × 3 runs = 72 questions per model; an independent judge model at temperature 0 with a reference-guided chain-of-thought protocol (solve from the source first, compare, then score) citing Zheng et al. 2023; criteria: groundedness, answer correctness, judge-solve match, distractor plausibility (1–5), overall quality (1–10); 95% bootstrap confidence intervals; latency (mean, p99, per question) and cost per 100 questions; failure analysis; a production default and a high-stakes fallback.
- Mock interviews (Section 6.8, FR-5.6, FR-5.7): the conversational model does not hold assessment authority; a separate terminal evaluation runs asynchronously on the stored transcript against teacher-defined outcomes (met / not met with stored reasoning); the student sees only pass/fail with a gap report; pending or failed evaluation never invents a verdict.
- Section 6.6.4: one LLM gateway over any OpenAI-compatible endpoint, model chosen per role, one audit row per call with tokens, cost and latency.
- Its judge was not validated against humans (no kappa or inter-rater analysis found), and Section 8.2 states this limitation for quiz quality.
