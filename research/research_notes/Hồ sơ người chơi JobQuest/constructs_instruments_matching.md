## 1. Which constructs carry the most useful self-knowledge per item, and what is the evidence linking each to choice, satisfaction or performance?

### Takeaway
Vocational interests are the best-supported construct for "where should I start exploring": they are more stable than personality from adolescence on, they have a public occupation database to match against, and interest–occupation congruence relates to performance (about .32) more than to satisfaction (about .19). Big Five traits predict how well people do across almost all jobs (conscientiousness) rather than which job they choose, so they add self-insight but little matching power; values, strengths and career adaptability have no open, short, occupation-linked instrument that is still maintained.

### Cited Findings
- **Interests → performance.** Meta-analysis of 60 studies and about 568 correlations: interests relate to performance and persistence in work and academic settings, and congruence indices correlate more strongly with performance than interest scores alone. Nye, C. D., Su, R., Rounds, J., & Drasgow, F. (2012). Vocational interests and performance: A quantitative summary of over 60 years of research. *Perspectives on Psychological Science, 7*(4), 384–403. doi:10.1177/1745691612449021 **[abstract]** — [Illinois Experts record](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/vocational-interests-and-performance-a-quantitative-summary-of-ov/)
- **Size of that effect.** Follow-up analysis of 92 studies and 1,858 correlations: baseline correlation with performance is 0.32 for interest congruence and 0.16 for interest scores alone. Nye, C. D., Su, R., Rounds, J., & Drasgow, F. (2017). Interest congruence and performance: Revisiting recent meta-analytic findings. *Journal of Vocational Behavior, 98*, 138–151. doi:10.1016/j.jvb.2016.11.002 **[abstract]** — [Illinois Experts record](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/interest-congruence-and-performance-revisiting-recent-meta-analyt/)
- **Interests → satisfaction is weak.** 105 studies (k = 194, N = 39,602): interest fit and overall job satisfaction ρ = 0.19 (95% CI 0.16–0.21); the relation is stronger for satisfaction with one's career choice in general. Lead author: interest fit "significantly predicts satisfaction, but it's not as strong of a relation as people expect." Hoff, K. A., Song, Q. C., Wee, C. J. M., Phan, W. M. J., & Rounds, J. (2020). Interest fit and job satisfaction: A systematic review and meta-analysis. *Journal of Vocational Behavior* (December 2020; volume and article number not confirmed in this session). **[abstract, seen via search excerpt; press release read in full]** — [University of Houston release](https://www.uh.edu/news-events/stories/2020/november-2020/11112020-kevin-hoff-interest-job-satisfaction)
- **Interests → actual career choice.** Almost 100 years of studies: measured interests reach an estimated overall hit rate of 50.8% in predicting career choice; accuracy varies by inventory, scale type, outcome type and hit-rate method. Hanna, A., & Rounds, J. (2020). How accurate are interest inventories? A quantitative review of career choice hit rates. *Psychological Bulletin, 146*(9), 765–796. doi:10.1037/bul0000269 **[abstract]** — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE:%22How%20accurate%20are%20interest%20inventories%22&resultType=core&format=json)
- **Interest stability in adolescents.** Longitudinal meta-analysis, ages 12–40: interest stability is substantial, unchanged through much of adolescence, rises sharply at ages 18–21.9 and stays high; interests are more rank-order stable than personality traits. Low, K. S. D., Yoon, M., Roberts, B. W., & Rounds, J. (2005). The stability of vocational interests from early adolescence to middle adulthood. *Psychological Bulletin, 131*(5), 713–737. doi:10.1037/0033-2909.131.5.713 **[abstract]** — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:16187855&resultType=core&format=json)
- **Big Five → performance.** Conscientiousness "showed consistent relations with all job performance criteria for all occupational groups"; extraversion predicted for managers and sales; openness and extraversion predicted training proficiency; for the remaining dimensions the estimated true-score correlations were small (ρ < .10). Barrick, M. R., & Mount, M. K. (1991). The Big Five personality dimensions and job performance: A meta-analysis. *Personnel Psychology, 44*(1), 1–26. doi:10.1111/j.1744-6570.1991.tb00688.x **[abstract]** — [University of Iowa record](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/THE-BIG-FIVE-PERSONALITY-DIMENSIONS-AND/9984963115202771)
- **Big Five ↔ interests overlap is partial.** In the O*NET Mini-IP validation sample, Social and Enterprising interests correlated with Extraversion (r = .28 and .34) and Investigative and Artistic with Openness (r = .15 and .35), described as very similar to prior meta-analyses (Barrick, Mount & Gupta, 2003; Larson, Rottinghaus & Borgen, 2002). Rounds, J., Wee, C. J. M., Cao, M., Song, C., & Lewis, P. (2016). *Development of an O\*NET Mini Interest Profiler (Mini-IP) for mobile devices: Psychometric characteristics.* National Center for O\*NET Development. **[full text, pp. 1–21; appendix tables not read]** — [report PDF](https://www.onetcenter.org/dl_files/Mini-IP.pdf)
- **Person–job fit → attitudes.** 172 studies, 836 effect sizes. Person–job fit: ρ = .56 with job satisfaction (k = 47, N = 12,960), .47 with organisational commitment, −.46 with intent to quit. Person–organisation fit with job satisfaction: ρ = .44 (k = 65). Within Table 1 the job-satisfaction estimate is .58 for directly *perceived* fit (k = 23) but .28 for *objective* indirect fit (k = 6, N = 531). Kristof-Brown, A. L., Zimmerman, R. D., & Johnson, E. C. (2005). Consequences of individuals' fit at work. *Personnel Psychology, 58*(2), 281–342. doi:10.1111/j.1744-6570.2005.00672.x **[full text, pp. 281–282 and 298–302 of a scanned copy; table rows read from a rotated scan, re-check before quoting]** — [scanned PDF](https://leeds-faculty.colorado.edu/dahe7472/kristof%20zimmerman%202005.pdf)
- **Work values.** O\*NET retired its Work Importance Locator on 3 June 2024 (materials archived "as-is", no technical assistance), and the Work Values file is "no longer updated or included in the O\*NET Database" from release 30.3 on; occupation pages no longer show a Work Values section. **[full text of both pages]** — [WIL page](https://www.onetcenter.org/WIP.html); [O\*NET 30.3 update appendix](https://www.onetcenter.org/dictionary/30.3/text/appendix_updates.html)
- **Career adaptability.** The CAAS measures concern, control, curiosity and confidence as "psychosocial resources for managing occupational transitions, developmental tasks, and work traumas" (4 scales × 6 items). Savickas, M. L., & Porfeli, E. J. (2012). Career Adapt-Abilities Scale: Construction, reliability, and measurement equivalence across 13 countries. *Journal of Vocational Behavior, 80*(3), 661–673. doi:10.1016/j.jvb.2012.01.011 **[abstract]** — [NEOMED repository record](https://neomed.omeka.net/items/show/7019)
- **Work styles as occupation descriptors.** O\*NET defines work styles as "personality tendencies exhibited at work, which can affect how well someone performs a job"; since release 30.1 there are 21 of them, rated per occupation on an Impact scale from −3.00 (very detrimental) to +3.00 (very beneficial). **[full text]** — [O\*NET 31.0 Work Styles file description](https://www.onetcenter.org/dictionary/31.0/excel/work_styles.html)

### Inferences
- Per item asked, interests give the most for JobQuest: one construct yields (a) a self-description, (b) a six-axis chart, (c) a link to a public occupation database. Nothing else on the list does all three.
- The evidence supports the product rule "suggestions are only starting points". A 50.8% hit rate and ρ = .19 with satisfaction mean an interest match is informative but wrong about as often as right for any one person. It does not support "you fit job X".
- The strong fit–satisfaction figure (.56) comes mostly from people rating their own fit after they are in the job; objectively computed fit is about half that (.28). A pre-play quiz is the weaker, objective-type match; the perceived fit that predicts satisfaction is what the user forms **by playing**. This is a good argument for the game's core loop.
- Big Five (especially conscientiousness) says "how you tend to work anywhere", not "which IT job". If shown, it belongs in the self-description, not in the matching.
- Career adaptability is a readiness construct. It fits the advisor's earlier "career maturity" suggestion as an optional before/after measure for evaluation, not as a profile-chart axis.
- The prototype's eight "work-style" dimensions are closest to O\*NET Work Styles/Work Context. They are legitimate as occupation descriptors and as signals collected from play, but there is no validated short self-report scale behind them.

### Gaps
- Character strengths (VIA): I did not open any source on licence or career-related validity in this session. Nothing is claimed.
- Rudolph, Lavigne, Katz & Zacher (2017, *JVB, 98*, 17–34) meta-analysis of career adaptability: citation located, findings not retrieved.
- I found no meta-analysis in this session that tests work-style *preferences* (as opposed to traits or interests) against occupational choice.
- Schwartz values and the theory of work adjustment were not researched beyond the O\*NET work-values status.

## 2. Short open instruments: items, licence, reliability, adolescent suitability, Vietnamese versions

### Takeaway
The O\*NET Mini-IP (30 items, CC BY 4.0 plus an O\*NET developer licence) is the only short, open, occupation-linked instrument found, with alphas of .74–.81 on five-item scales and 73% first-letter agreement with the 60-item form. No validated Vietnamese version was found for any instrument on the list; whatever the team uses will be a self-translation that must be labelled unvalidated.

### Inferences
- For a five-minute, phone-first onboarding only three candidates are short enough and open enough: Mini-IP (30 interest items), Mini-IPIP (20 personality items) and TIPI (10). Only the Mini-IP links to occupations.
- The Mini-IP's known weakness for JobQuest is content, not length: its Investigative items are all natural-science activities and its Realistic items are manual trades. A 15-year-old who loves coding but not chemistry can score low on Investigative. The instrument was built to separate IT from nursing, not backend from data.
- Adolescent evidence is thinner than it looks: the Short Form sample included high schools, but the Mini-IP validation used working adults only, and the "14+" guidance is from a secondary source. Interest scores at 12–18 are also less stable than after 18, so a 15-year-old's profile should be presented as "for now".
- Using the O\*NET items translated into Vietnamese is permitted, but it triggers the modified-version notice and, on my reading, the licence's validation-study expectation. Writing original items "in the style of" RIASEC avoids the licence but has no psychometric backing. Either way the report must say "self-translated/self-written, not validated" (this is the existing open issue 07 C6).
- HEXACO is unsuitable: its free licence is limited to non-profit academic research with access-restricted online use, which a public web game does not meet.
- A Vietnamese HEXACO item file and a Vietnamese IPIP-NEO-120 exist as translations; neither was found with a validation study, so they do not solve the validation gap.
- Likert personality items carry an acquiescence risk for the youngest users; a like/dislike activity format (as in the Interest Profiler) or balanced keying is safer than agree/disagree trait statements.

### Gaps
- IPIP official site could not be opened (DNS error). The public-domain statement rests on Goldberg et al. (2006) plus a search excerpt of the site.
- BFI-2 terms of use and translation list: page returned 403; not verified.
- CAAS/CAAS-SF licence terms: not found.

## 3. Within IT: O*NET interest codes, work styles and work values for eleven occupations

### Takeaway
All eleven IT occupations are Investigative + Conventional at the top and uniformly low on Social (9–28 of 100), so a RIASEC three-letter code or a People–Things axis cannot separate most of them. What does separate them is Enterprising (10–82), Artistic (0–58), Realistic (12–53), the balance of Investigative versus Conventional, a handful of work styles (Leadership, Social Orientation, Cautiousness, Integrity, Innovation, Cooperation) and O\*NET's finer "Specific Interest Areas".

## 4. Sex differences on People–Things and what they imply for fairness

### Takeaway
People–Things is the largest known sex difference in interests (d = 0.93) and even the O\*NET short forms keep d ≈ .86 on Realistic and ≈ −.59 on Social, so any suggestion rule that leans on this axis will hand different job groups to girls and boys on average. Within IT the axis also has little discriminating value (section 3), so it costs fairness without buying accuracy.

### Cited Findings
- Technical manuals of 47 interest inventories, 503,188 respondents: Things–People d = 0.93 ("men prefer working with things and women prefer working with people"); Realistic d = 0.84 and Investigative d = 0.26 favouring men; Artistic d = −0.35, Social d = −0.68, Conventional d = −0.33 favouring women; engineering d = 1.11, science 0.36, mathematics 0.34. Average effects differed across inventories from 0.08 to 0.79, and "application of some item development strategies can substantially reduce sex differences". Su, R., Rounds, J., & Armstrong, P. I. (2009). Men and things, women and people: A meta-analysis of sex differences in interests. *Psychological Bulletin, 135*(6), 859–884. doi:10.1037/a0017364 **[abstract; the Enterprising value is not in the abstract]** — [University of Iowa record](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Men-and-things-women-and-people/9984380502202771)
- O\*NET Short Form: Realistic d = .86 and Investigative d = .26 (men higher); Social d = −.59 and Conventional d = −.36 (women higher); Artistic d = .00, Enterprising d = −.07. Rounds et al. (2010). **[full text]** — [report PDF](https://www.onetcenter.org/dl_files/IPSF_Psychometric.pdf)
- O\*NET Mini-IP: items were chosen partly to keep gender differences per scale "acceptably small"; the developmental sample still shows Realistic d = .86 and Social d = .59; in the validation sample the Conventional difference was .00. Rounds et al. (2016). **[full text]** — [report PDF](https://www.onetcenter.org/dl_files/Mini-IP.pdf)
- Across STEM fields the share of women correlates with the field's people-orientation (r = 0.72) and things-orientation (r = −0.66), and with the sex difference in interests (r = −0.89); required quantitative ability did not explain the pattern. For computer science the article's Table 4 gives an interest difference of d = 0.38 (one inventory), things-orientation 4.51 and people-orientation 1.76 on a 1–7 scale, 23.75% women. Orientation was taken from O\*NET Realistic and Social occupation scores. Su, R., & Rounds, J. (2015). All STEM fields are not created equal: People and things interests explain gender disparities across STEM fields. *Frontiers in Psychology, 6*, 189. doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00189 **[full text, first ~100,000 characters]** — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00189/full)

### Inferences
- If "works with people" or a People–Things score is weighted in the matcher, girls will on average be steered toward the management/BA/design group and boys toward infrastructure and QA. That reproduces the existing composition of the field in a tool meant to open options for 15-year-olds.
- The Realistic scale is the main carrier (d ≈ .86), and its item content is manual trades (kitchen cabinets, appliance repair, truck driving). Those items measure liking for trades more than liking for servers or devices. The 2009 meta-analysis says item choice can shrink the gap, which supports rewriting the "things" items around IT hardware and systems if the team writes its own items.
- Safer design, in order of effect: (1) do not use a single People–Things axis for suggestions; (2) match on the full profile, centred within IT, so that no single sex-linked scale dominates; (3) never apply sex-specific norms and do not ask for gender at all (also consistent with data minimisation); (4) always show at least one "different from your profile" group as an invitation to test; (5) keep the suggestion a ranking of starting points with all first levels open, which the product already does.
- If gender is not collected, the team cannot audit the suggestion distribution by gender. A one-off pilot with consenting testers who state gender would be the way to check, outside the product.

### Gaps
- Full text of Su et al. (2009) not read: moderator results by age and by item-development strategy are known only at abstract level.
- No Vietnamese data on sex differences in interests was found; the Mendeley dataset (section 2) might allow it but was not opened.
- No study was found on whether interest-based recommender output changes girls' exploration behaviour; the fairness concern is an inference from mean differences.

## 5. How accurate is self-assessment of one's own abilities, and what may a self-reported "aptitude" indicator claim?

### Takeaway
Self-assessed ability correlates only about .29 with measured performance on average, and less when the self-rating is broad and the task is unfamiliar, which is exactly the situation of a student rating abilities for jobs never tried. A self-reported indicator can honestly be called confidence or self-belief; it cannot be called aptitude.

### Cited Findings
- Metasynthesis of 22 meta-analyses: mean correlation between ability self-evaluations and performance M = .29 (SD = .11), individual meta-analytic effects from .09 to .63; the relation is stronger when self-evaluations are "specific to a given domain rather than broad" and when tasks are objective, familiar or low in complexity. Domains covered include academic ability, intelligence, language competence, medical skills, sports ability and vocational skills. Zell, E., & Krizan, Z. (2014). Do people have insight into their abilities? A metasynthesis. *Perspectives on Psychological Science, 9*(2), 111–125. doi:10.1177/1745691613518075 **[abstract]** — [APS journal page](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/perspectives/1745691613518075/)

### Inferences
- r = .29 means self-rating explains under a tenth of the variation in performance. Granting points, unlocks or "aptitude" labels from it would contradict BR-02 on evidence grounds, not only on policy grounds.
- The moderators point the wrong way for onboarding: the user is rating broad abilities for unfamiliar, complex work. Accuracy should be expected at the low end of the .09–.63 span.
- The same moderators point the right way for the game: after playing, self-ratings become specific, about familiar and objective tasks. A "how do you think you did?" prompt after a scenario, compared with the scored result, is a legitimate calibration feature and real self-knowledge.
- Honest labels: "Mức tự tin" (confidence) or "Mình nghĩ mình mạnh ở…" (self-view). Honest claim: "this is what you believe now; play will show what you can do".

### Gaps
- Full text not read; which domains are most and least accurate is not in the abstract.
- The companion commentary (Dunning & Helzer, 2014, same issue, pp. 126–130) on limits of the correlation as an accuracy measure was located but not read.
- Nothing adolescent-specific or IT-specific on self-assessment accuracy was retrieved.

## 6. Type labels versus continuous traits: MBTI evidence, what 16Personalities uses, how many dimensions lay users absorb

### Takeaway
The peer-reviewed evidence found says the MBTI measures four continuous dimensions that overlap with four of the Big Five, not sixteen distinct types. 16Personalities describes its own model as five trait spectrums presented with type-style letter codes. No study was found on how many dimensions a lay user can absorb, so "five or six axes" is a design judgement, not an evidence-based number.

### Cited Findings
- 267 men and 201 women aged 19–93, MBTI and NEO-PI with self-reports and peer ratings: no support for the view that the MBTI measures truly dichotomous preferences or qualitatively distinct types; it measures four relatively independent dimensions; the four indices measured aspects of four of the five major dimensions of normal personality; the authors propose the five-factor model as the framework for interpreting MBTI findings. McCrae, R. R., & Costa, P. T., Jr. (1989). Reinterpreting the Myers-Briggs Type Indicator from the perspective of the five-factor model of personality. *Journal of Personality, 57*(1), 17–40. doi:10.1111/j.1467-6494.1989.tb00759.x **[abstract]** — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE:%22Reinterpreting%20the%20Myers-Briggs%20Type%20Indicator%22&resultType=core&format=json)
- 16Personalities states that it uses its own "NERIS" framework with five independent trait spectrums; the first four letters form the familiar sixteen codes and a fifth trait, Identity, adds Assertive/Turbulent variants (e.g. INTJ-A, INTJ-T); it keeps the Myers-Briggs acronym format but does not use Jungian cognitive functions, and asks not to be treated as interchangeable with the MBTI. **[secondary: search-engine excerpts of the site's own pages; the pages returned 403]** — [16Personalities AI reference page](https://www.16personalities.com/for-ai)
- Lay readers do not interpret Big Five trait labels exactly as the scales define them: free-response descriptions of people high on each trait showed "substantial commonality but also notable areas of non-overlap" with standard scale content (Hall et al., 2019). **[secondary: search excerpt]** — [University of Bern repository record](https://boris.unibe.ch/143365/)

### Inferences
- The project report's criticism of type labels is consistent with McCrae & Costa (1989): the data are continuous, so a cut at the midpoint invents a category. JobQuest can show continuous bars or a radar and avoid any four-letter or animal-style label without losing information.
- 16Personalities is evidence that users like type-style presentation, not evidence that types exist. If the team wants the "character" feeling, a descriptive title generated from the top one or two indicators ("thiên về tìm tòi và ngăn nắp") keeps the continuous model underneath and does not claim a category.
- Because lay readers reinterpret trait words, indicator names should describe an activity ("thích tìm hiểu vì sao") rather than a trait noun ("Investigative", "hướng nội"), and each should carry a one-line plain meaning.

### Gaps
- Pittenger (2005), "Cautionary comments regarding the Myers-Briggs Type Indicator", *Consulting Psychology Journal, 57*(3), 210–221 (the report's reference [6]): abstract could not be opened; no reliability figure is quoted from it here. The common "about half change type on retest" claim was **not** verified in this session.
- Whether 16Personalities' five scales are a relabelled Big Five is asserted by third parties; the site's own "our theory" page could not be opened to confirm its wording. No independent reliability/validity study of the NERIS test was found.
- No empirical source was found on how many profile dimensions lay users or adolescents can understand or remember. Searches returned only adjacent work (Barnum effect, label interpretation).
- DISC and Enneagram evidence was not researched.

## 7. Person–environment fit and interest congruence: how strong is the link, so that suggestions are worded honestly?

### Takeaway
Congruence between a person's interests and an occupation is a real but modest signal: about .32 with performance, .19 with job satisfaction, and roughly a 51% hit rate for predicting the career actually chosen. Wording should therefore be "a place to start trying", with the reason shown and an explicit invitation to test the opposite.

### Cited Findings
- Interest congruence and performance: baseline correlation 0.32 (interest scores alone 0.16), 92 studies, 1,858 correlations. Nye et al. (2017). **[abstract]** — [Illinois Experts record](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/interest-congruence-and-performance-revisiting-recent-meta-analyt/)
- Interests relate to persistence as well as performance in work and academic settings. Nye et al. (2012). **[abstract; no effect size for persistence in the abstract]** — [Illinois Experts record](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/vocational-interests-and-performance-a-quantitative-summary-of-ov/)
- Interest fit and job satisfaction: ρ = 0.19 (95% CI 0.16–0.21), k = 194, N = 39,602; stronger for satisfaction with career choice in general; the lead author adds that the organisation, supervisor, colleagues and pay also drive satisfaction. Hoff et al. (2020). **[abstract via search excerpt; press release read]** — [University of Houston release](https://www.uh.edu/news-events/stories/2020/november-2020/11112020-kevin-hoff-interest-job-satisfaction)
- Career-choice hit rate of interest inventories: 50.8% overall. Hanna & Rounds (2020). **[abstract]** — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE:%22How%20accurate%20are%20interest%20inventories%22&resultType=core&format=json)
- Perceived person–job fit relates to job satisfaction at ρ = .58 while objectively assessed fit relates at .28. Kristof-Brown et al. (2005), Table 1. **[full text, scanned table]** — [scanned PDF](https://leeds-faculty.colorado.edu/dahe7472/kristof%20zimmerman%202005.pdf)
- Two short forms of the *same* inventory give the same top letter for 73% of people (Mini-IP versus Short Form); the Short Form agrees with the Long Form at κ = .74 and with another RIASEC inventory at κ = .59, and agreement between other major inventories ranges from κ = .26 to .52. Rounds et al. (2016); Rounds et al. (2010). **[full text]** — [Mini-IP report](https://www.onetcenter.org/dl_files/Mini-IP.pdf); [Short Form report](https://www.onetcenter.org/dl_files/IPSF_Psychometric.pdf)

### Inferences
- A top-letter result is itself unstable across instruments (roughly one person in four gets a different first letter from a 30-item versus 60-item version). Suggestions should be driven by the whole profile and shown at group level (five or so job groups), not as a ranked list of single jobs with a percentage.
- Do not show a "match %". Nothing in the evidence supports precision of that kind, and a number invites the reading "you fit job X".
- Honest sentence patterns (the Vietnamese versions are in the last section): "Based on what you said you like, these groups are a good place to try first"; "People whose interests line up with a job tend to do somewhat better in it; whether they enjoy it depends on much more"; "Try one group that looks unlike you, then compare how the two felt".
- Since perceived fit is the stronger correlate of satisfaction, the profile page should, after play, ask the user how well each tried role suited them and show that next to the quiz-based suggestion. That is the part of the profile that should grow.

### Gaps
- No effect size for interest congruence and persistence (staying in a major or job) was read; only the direction is in the abstract.
- No evidence specific to IT occupations or to Vietnamese samples on congruence outcomes was found.
- Van Iddekinge et al. (2011), the competing meta-analysis that Nye et al. (2017) responds to, was not read.
- Prediger (1982), *Journal of Vocational Behavior, 21*, 259–287: located as a citation only; the formulas that convert RIASEC scores to Data–Ideas and Things–People were not verified from a primary source in this session, so none is given here.

Chính các sở thích cụ thể này mới tách được nghề IT (ví dụ Toán/Thống kê: 24 ở quản trị mạng, 98 ở khoa học dữ liệu).
- Báo cáo ghi rõ: bản dịch và câu tự viết **chưa kiểm định** (vẫn là 07 C6). Việc tối thiểu nên làm: thử trên 30–50 người, tính α từng trục và cho làm lại sau hai tuần.
- Không dùng HEXACO (chỉ miễn phí cho nghiên cứu học thuật phi lợi nhuận, không được để công khai). Nếu muốn có vài câu tính cách cho đoạn mô tả thì lấy từ IPIP (miền công cộng), và chỉ dùng cho mô tả, không dùng để ghép nghề.
- Chữ tự do theo hướng của Phúc: dùng để viết đoạn mô tả và để hỏi tiếp. Điểm sáu trục nên đến từ câu chọn nhanh, vì chưa có thang nào đã kiểm định cho việc đọc chữ tự do ra điểm sở thích.

**4. Ra gợi ý nhóm nghề thế nào.**
1. Gán mỗi nghề trong 22 nghề một mã O\*NET-SOC, lấy hồ sơ sáu điểm 0–100 (Career Interest Types) từ O\*NET 31.0. Nghề nào không có mã khớp thì ghi rõ là nhóm tự ước lượng.
2. **Không so mã ba chữ**: 6 trong 11 nghề đã tra là IC hoặc CI nên sẽ hoà. So cả sáu điểm.
3. **Chuẩn hoá trong tập IT**: trừ trung bình của 22 nghề trên từng trục, để phần chung "IT nào cũng Tìm tòi và Ngăn nắp" không lấn át. Hồ sơ người chơi cũng trừ trung bình của chính họ. Tính tương quan giữa hai hồ sơ.
4. Gộp kết quả theo **năm nhóm nghề**, hiện hai hoặc ba nhóm đứng đầu kèm lý do bằng chữ, **không hiện phần trăm khớp**, và luôn kèm một nhóm "khác bạn nhất" để mời thử:
   - Xây sản phẩm (backend, frontend, mobile, game, QA): Tìm tòi và Ngăn nắp cao, Dẫn dắt thấp.
   - Dữ liệu và AI: Tìm tòi cao nhất, thích toán và thống kê.
   - Hạ tầng và bảo mật (mạng, DevOps, cloud, DBA, bảo mật, hỗ trợ): Ngăn nắp và Máy móc cao, cẩn trọng.
   - Thiết kế và trải nghiệm: Sáng tạo cao.
   - Sản phẩm, nghiệp vụ và quản lý (BA, PM, quản lý): Dẫn dắt cao, Kết nối cao hơn các nhóm khác.
5. Không hỏi giới tính, không dùng chuẩn theo giới. Trục Máy móc và Kết nối chênh theo giới lớn (d khoảng 0,86 và 0,59 ngay trong bộ O\*NET), nên không để một trục đơn lẻ quyết định gợi ý.
6. Sau khi chơi, hỏi người chơi "vai này hợp bạn tới đâu" và hiện cạnh gợi ý ban đầu. Mức hợp do chính người chơi cảm nhận liên quan tới sự hài lòng mạnh hơn hẳn mức hợp tính từ bài hỏi (0,58 so với 0,28).

**5. Lời gợi ý nên viết thế nào.** "Dựa trên điều bạn nói bạn thích, đây là mấy nhóm nên thử trước." Không viết "bạn hợp nghề X". Có thể nói thật với người chơi: bài hỏi kiểu này đoán đúng nghề người ta chọn khoảng một nửa số lần (50,8%), và sở thích khớp nghề chỉ liên quan yếu tới việc có hài lòng hay không (ρ = 0,19). Vì vậy phải chơi thử mới biết.

**6. Về "năng khiếu".** Không gọi là năng khiếu và không tặng điểm. Tự đánh giá năng lực chỉ tương quan khoảng 0,29 với kết quả thật, và còn thấp hơn khi đánh giá chung chung về việc chưa từng làm. Nếu muốn giữ một chỉ số tự khai thì gọi là **"Mức tự tin"**, hiện tách khỏi điểm năng lực, và dùng nó cho một việc có ích: sau mỗi lượt chơi đặt cạnh nhau "bạn nghĩ mình làm tới đâu" và "bạn đã làm tới đâu". Khoảng cách giữa hai con số đó mới là hiểu biết về bản thân mà người chơi nhận được.

**7. Ba việc cần kiểm lại trước khi đưa vào báo cáo.**
- Số O\*NET trong ghi chú này đọc từ trang web qua công cụ trích; tải file O\*NET 31.0 và đối chiếu.
- Hồ sơ sở thích và work styles của O\*NET hiện do mô hình máy học và AI kết hợp chuyên gia tạo ra, cho nghề ở Mỹ. Ghi rõ giới hạn này.
- Con số "một nửa người làm lại MBTI đổi loại" chưa được kiểm trong phiên này; nếu báo cáo đang dùng thì mở lại Pittenger (2005) hoặc dẫn McCrae và Costa (1989) cho ý "MBTI đo bốn chiều liên tục, không phải mười sáu loại".
